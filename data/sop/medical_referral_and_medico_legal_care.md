# Triage first

If the caller reports current serious injury, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, poisoning, sexual violence requiring urgent care, risk of self-harm, or another medical emergency, direct them to local emergency services or the nearest emergency department and activate the critical escalation pathway. Do not delay emergency care to complete reporting, collect evidence, or obtain legal documents.

# Safe referral and consent

- Ask only what is needed to arrange safe care: approximate location, immediate symptoms, mobility/access needs, whether a trusted person can accompany the caller, and a safe way to contact them.
- Offer an emergency department, government health facility, or another clinically appropriate local service using an approved directory. Do not diagnose, prescribe, assess injury severity remotely, or tell the caller not to seek care.
- Obtain consent before sharing identifiable information with a health service, except where a separate lawful emergency protocol requires otherwise. Respect a caller's decision about accompaniment and communication where safely possible.

# Medico-legal documentation

Government medico-legal guidance places life-saving treatment and stabilisation before medico-legal formalities. A caller may seek treatment even if they have not filed an FIR; hospital and jurisdictional requirements govern examination, police notification, records, and consent. The operator must not advise a caller to delay treatment, wash or preserve items in a particular way, or make clinical/legal promises about evidence.

If the caller asks about documentation, explain that the treating facility can explain its medico-legal process and that the caller can request information about records and referrals. Record the facility referral and the caller's stated concern, but do not copy medical details into the general case narrative unless necessary for safety and the caller consents.

# Privacy, dignity, and follow-up

Use the minimum necessary health information, restrict access to authorised staff, and avoid sensitive details in unencrypted or shared channels. Offer a follow-up only with consent, using the agreed safe contact method. If intimidation, transport, disability access, or cost prevents care, escalate to a human operator for local support and legal-aid referral.

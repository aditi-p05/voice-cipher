# When to offer legal-aid referral

Offer a legal-aid referral when a caller asks about reporting, an FIR, bail or court updates, protection, compensation/relief, documents, or representation; also offer it when the caller reports an atrocity, discrimination, violence, intimidation, or difficulty accessing justice. Do not decide legal eligibility beyond the information in this SOP and do not give legal advice about the merits of a case.

# Eligibility and access

The National Legal Services Authority states that members of Scheduled Castes or Scheduled Tribes, and people affected by caste atrocity, may be entitled to free legal services under the Legal Services Authorities Act framework. NALSA describes applications through the nearest Legal Services Authority, including District Legal Services Authorities, and notes that an application can be made in writing, online, or orally with assistance from a paralegal volunteer or authority officer. Eligibility and documentary requirements must be confirmed by the receiving Legal Services Authority.

# Referral procedure

- Ask for the caller's district and preferred language, then identify the relevant District or State Legal Services Authority using an approved, current directory.
- Obtain consent before sharing any case details. Provide the caller with the official contact route and, if consented, send a minimum-necessary referral that identifies the urgent issue and safe callback method.
- Explain that legal-aid staff, not the helpline, assess eligibility, assign counsel, and advise on the case. Never promise representation, compensation, a court result, or a deadline.
- If the caller has immediate safety concerns, complete the critical escalation pathway first; legal-aid referral does not replace emergency response.

# Support for participation and protection

For a possible SC/ST Act matter, record requests or concerns about safety, intimidation, travel, rehabilitation, accessibility, language, or being informed about proceedings. Section 15A describes rights relating to dignity, protection, notice, and being heard; route these concerns to the designated human operator and legal-aid contact. Do not disclose the caller's address or witness information beyond the authorised referral.

# Handoff and follow-up

Record the authority referred to, date/time, consent status, information shared, the caller's preferred contact method, and any reason a referral could not be completed. Arrange follow-up only with explicit consent and never leave sensitive details in voicemail, SMS, or shared devices without agreement.

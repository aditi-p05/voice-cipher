# Scope and safety boundary

Use this SOP when a caller reports conduct that may be an offence, including a possible offence under the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. It is an operator aid, not a legal opinion or a finding that an offence occurred. Do not require the caller to prove caste status, identify every legal section, produce documents, or repeat traumatic details before recording the report and arranging the appropriate referral.

# Immediate intake and preservation

- First ask whether anyone is in immediate danger, injured, being threatened, or unable to reach a safe place. If yes, use the critical escalation SOP before continuing routine intake.
- Record the caller's preferred safe contact method, language, location, alleged incident time/place, persons at risk, and a concise account in the caller's own words. Capture only information necessary for the referral; do not place caste, medical, contact, or identity details in an open or public channel.
- Ask whether there are time-sensitive records the caller wants preserved, such as messages, call logs, photographs, video, medical papers, or witness details. Do not alter, upload publicly, or direct the caller to confront anyone. Explain that the receiving authority decides what evidence it needs.

# FIR and police referral pathway

- Offer to connect the caller with the appropriate local police station or designated police contact. With the caller's consent, share the minimum necessary incident summary and safe contact details.
- Clearly record whether the caller reports that an FIR/complaint has already been made, the police station, date, diary/FIR number if known, and any barrier reported. Do not invent an FIR number or represent that registration has occurred until confirmed by the receiving authority or the caller.
- If the caller reports refusal, delay, intimidation, or inability to safely approach the station, escalate to the designated supervisor/legal-aid pathway and document the reported barrier verbatim. The operator must not promise a legal outcome, arrest, charge, or protection order.

# Victim and witness safeguards

Section 15A of the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act provides for protection of victims, dependants, and witnesses, fair and dignified treatment, notice of proceedings, and possible protection/rehabilitation measures through the competent authorities. Ask whether the caller fears retaliation or witness intimidation, record the immediate safety concern, and route it for urgent human review. Keep the case restricted to authorised staff and minimise disclosure of names and addresses.

# Operator handoff record

Before closing the interaction, document: risk level; consent and any limits on sharing; referral made; contact/agency details; time and outcome of the handoff; follow-up owner; and unresolved safety or access barriers. Tell the caller how to seek emergency help locally if danger changes. A human operator must review all legal or police referrals.

# Sources

- India Code, *Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989*, especially section 15A (rights of victims and witnesses).
- Use current State/UT police, Special Court, and district administration procedures for local routing and relief processes.
